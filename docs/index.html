Design rules

One owner

Every controlled target has one normal command owner.

Semantic intent

Policies request outcomes rather than transport operations.

Observed completion

A command acknowledgement never substitutes for target state.

Bounded recovery

Retries, timers, traces and failure paths remain finite.

Next

Device-agnostic core

Separate decision logic from devices. Policies and controllers speak a neutral vocabulary of targets, intents and observations; a translation layer maps them to specific protocols, vendors and dashboards. Each part stays usable on its own, defaults name no rooms or devices, and powered devices are modelled from observed power.

In progress

Home Assistant integration

Home Assistant is an optional view and control panel: each node can publish its state and announce entities, and Home Assistant controls send MQTT messages that the nodes act on, never commands to devices. Shipped: opt-in state publishing and discovery, a working circadian switch and no room defaults. Next: availability, stable entity identifiers that survive renames, cleanup of removed entities, and entities for sleep, wake and bed times, occupancy, motion and receiver volume.

In progress

Blind and cover control

Covers are getting their own nodes. A cover controller owns one blind, shutter or curtain motor: positions and named presets, open/close-only motors judged honestly from what they report, a moving signal other policies can react to, and a person's move left where they put it. A cover group moves several covers as one and says which confirmed. Next: Home Assistant cover entities.

Planned

Scenes with restore

A scene temporarily owns a set of targets, such as lights or motion enablement, and records their observed state. Ending it, by request, a safety timer or a configured trigger, restores every target. Overlapping scenes stack: the newest owns shared targets and hands them back to the one beneath.

Local resilience

Define deterministic fallback behavior for temporary controller, broker or transport loss without creating a second command owner.

Planned

Water irrigation

Run garden zones from schedules and soil or rain evidence, one valve owner per zone. Each run is bounded, confirmed from the valve's observed state, and skipped or cut short when rain or moisture says it isn't needed. A person's manual run takes over until it ends.

Planned

Heating and climate

Hold each room at a scheduled temperature from observed temperature and valve state. A person's manual change wins until the next schedule step, and an open window pauses heating instead of fighting it.

Planned

Humidity-driven ventilation

Run extractor fans in bathrooms and kitchens from a rise in humidity and stop them once it falls back, with a bounded run time, a manual boost and a short overrun.

Planned

Water leak protection

Close a main water valve when a leak sensor trips and confirm it closed from the valve's observed state. The alarm stays latched until a person clears it, and an unconfirmed close is reported, never assumed.

Planned

Energy-aware scheduling

Run deferrable loads, such as a dishwasher, water heating or vehicle charging, in the cheapest or greenest window from tariff or solar data, finishing before a deadline the user sets.
